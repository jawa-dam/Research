We're looking for expertise—not agreement.

GEI invites scholars, professionals, independent researchers, and curious specialists to examine a proposed interpretation of Genesis 1–6 through linguistic, textual, hydraulic, engineering, and archaeological questions. If your expertise fits one of the seven disciplines, there's a place to start.

We're not looking for people who believe the blueprint. We're looking for people qualified to examine it.

You bring the expertise. We'll bring the blueprint. Challenge the blueprint. Agreement is not required.

Research integrity notice: GEI is not presented as academic consensus, an established Hebrew translation, proven ancient hydraulic technology, or confirmed archaeological discovery. This initiative distinguishes observation, analogy, hypothesis, evidence, and unresolved questions. Critical review and corrections are invited.

Seven Disciplines, One Investigation

Each pathway has a distinct research question. Specialists are invited to examine only the questions within their expertise—agreement with GEI is neither expected nor required.

THE LANGUAGE INVESTIGATION

Hebrew Scholars

Help distinguish linguistic evidence from linguistic speculation. Review Hebrew vocabulary, morphology, syntax, semantic range, proposed word associations, and departures from established scholarship.

EXAMINE THE HEBREW

THE TEXTUAL INVESTIGATION

Biblical Researchers

Review Genesis 1–6 literary structure, sequence, genre, translation choices, contextual interpretation, canonical context, alternative readings, and whether GEI mappings follow from the text.

EXAMINE THE TEXT

THE STRUCTURAL INVESTIGATION

Civil Engineers

Review physical coherence of the proposed containment system: foundations, dam/reservoir arrangement, outlets, channels, loads, materials, elevation, and structural sequencing.

TEST THE STRUCTURE

THE WATER INVESTIGATION

Hydrologists

Review watershed logic, collection, drainage, water balance, storage, controlled release, flow direction, elevation/head, and whether proposed sequences resemble recognizable water-management principles.

INVESTIGATE THE FLOW

THE ANCIENT TECHNOLOGY INVESTIGATION

Archaeologists

Review securely dated evidence for ancient dams, canals, reservoirs, irrigation, mills, and water-control technologies. Evaluate chronology, geography, cultural context, and what physical evidence would be required.

EXAMINE THE ANCIENT EVIDENCE

THE MECHANICAL INVESTIGATION

Mechanical Engineers

Review proposed waterwheel/mill connections, operating principles, flow and head requirements, power transfer, shafts/gearing, and mechanical plausibility.

TEST THE MECHANISM

THE COMPARATIVE TRADITIONS INVESTIGATION

Ancient Near Eastern Scholars

Examine relevant Mesopotamian, Egyptian, and other ancient creation traditions; assess source languages, textual histories, chronology, cultural context, and meaningful differences as well as similarities with Genesis.

Researchers do not need to evaluate the entire GEI framework. Each contributor can examine a defined question within their own discipline.

PATH ASpecialist contributors

Research contributors

For scholars, professionals, students, and independent researchers with relevant expertise.

Possible contributions: linguistic review, textual analysis, engineering assessment, hydrological evaluation, archaeological sources, mechanical calculations, and comparative textual research.

PATH BCommunity participation

Research supporters

For interested readers, question-raisers, source finders, and people following the investigation.

Possible contributions: questions, source suggestions, constructive critiques, corrections, and interest in public research updates.

The Shared Review Process

01

Define

Identify a precise research question and the discipline qualified to examine it.

02

Investigate

Record evidence, sources, assumptions, methods, and alternative explanations.

03

Challenge

Document objections, corrections, limitations, and what could weaken the hypothesis.

04

Synthesize

Compare specialist findings without treating one discipline's review as approval of the whole model.

The question every reviewer should ask

“What would have to be true for this interpretation to be wrong?”

GEI welcomes evidence that supports, refines, limits, or challenges its proposals. Negative findings and unresolved questions are part of the research record.

What a research application should include

  • Name or preferred display name and a private contact email
  • Primary discipline and optional secondary discipline
  • Optional title, affiliation, ORCID, or professional profile
  • Relevant experience and the question you would like to examine
  • Preferred participation: specialist contributor or research supporter
  • Separate, optional permission for public attribution

Privacy: Contact details and private submissions should not be posted in public comments. Public attribution must be opt-in. No home address, date of birth, phone number, or government identification is needed.

Application status: received · under review · accepted · waitlisted · declined · withdrawn. Submission status and application status are tracked separately in the planned private workspace.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is GEI?
Genesis Engineered Interpretation (GEI) is an alternative interpretive framework that examines Genesis 1–6 through proposed linguistic, textual, hydraulic, archaeological, and mechanical-engineering comparisons. It is a research hypothesis, not an established academic position.
Do I need academic credentials to participate?
No institutional affiliation or formal credentials are required to express interest. We welcome independent researchers, professionals, students, and curious minds. However, technical contributions will be evaluated on their merits and methodology.
Is GEI claiming to be proven science?
No. GEI distinguishes between observations, analogies, hypotheses, and evidence. The initiative exists specifically to invite critical examination — agreement is not required or expected.
What happens after I apply?
The separate GEI research-interest form is being prepared. It will be activated only after a dedicated private form endpoint has been created and tested. Until then, please do not post private contact details in public GitHub issues or use the ordinary YallToo support form for research inquiries.
Can I just follow the research without contributing?
Yes. The Evidence Room and research library are publicly accessible. A separate research-interest form will provide a way to follow updates without mixing research inquiries with ordinary YallToo support.
