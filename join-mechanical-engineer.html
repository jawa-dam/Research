⚙️ Machines & power division

Welcome, Mechanical Engineer.

If GEI proposes a waterwheel or mill relationship, the mechanism must make sense. Bring your knowledge of machines, motion, power transfer, shafts, gearing, flow, and mechanical limits. A machine that cannot work is a useful finding.

THE MECHANISM CASE FILE

Your investigation assignment

Examine proposed waterwheel and mill analogies, including flow and head requirements, torque, power transfer, shafts, gearing, materials, and operating constraints.

Your first challenge

Under what physical conditions could the proposed water-driven mechanism function—and what assumptions make it fail?

What you can bring to the crew

  • Mechanical engineering, fluid machinery, machine design, or energy systems knowledge
  • Calculations or practical understanding of torque, power, flow, and mechanisms
  • A willingness to explain why a proposed mechanism is incomplete or impossible

What you may gain

  • A distinctive engineering question to investigate
  • A chance to connect mechanical principles with historical technology questions
  • A documented contribution that values negative results as much as positive ones

How the crew works

Choose a specific question. Examine sources or assumptions. Record what supports or challenges the proposal. Share what you find with clarity and respect.

No agreement required
Negative findings welcome

Your role matters

You're not here to defend a conclusion.

GEI is a proposed interpretive framework under investigation. Your contribution may support a comparison, narrow its limits, identify a better explanation, or show that an idea does not hold up. All of those outcomes are useful.
